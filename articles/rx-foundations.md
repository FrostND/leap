# Research Foundations

## Overview

Below is a selected bibliography of research relevant to defining and
analyzing treatment episodes in longitudinal behavioral health data. The
literature is organized around two related methodological issues: how
distinct episodes of care have been identified in longitudinal treatment
records and how treatment outcomes have been analyzed when individuals
contribute repeated episodes of care.

### Time-based episode definitions

A large number of intervention studies have used the time between
treatment contacts to distinguish separate episodes of behavioral health
care. In psychotherapy research, a gap of at least 90 days without
treatment has frequently been used to identify the beginning of a new
episode of care. This approach provides a practical and clinically
interpretable rule for distinguishing relatively short interruptions in
ongoing treatment from subsequent reengagement.

Importantly, many studies using time-based episode definitions have not
subsequently analyzed all identified episodes. Instead, episode
delimiters have often been used to identify distinct periods of care
before selecting a single episode—such as the first or most recent
episode—for the primary analysis. These studies nevertheless provide
important methodological precedent for treating sufficiently long gaps
between sessions as evidence of distinct treatment episodes.

### Analysis of Repeated Episodes

A separate body of research has examined clients who engage in more than
one episode of treatment. Definitions of an episode vary considerably
across this literature, and many studies rely on retrospective reports
of previous treatment experiences rather than longitudinal records of
care. Consequently, although repeated treatment utilization has received
substantial attention, relatively few studies have identified and
analyzed multiple episodes of care for the same clients within a single
longitudinal data set.
